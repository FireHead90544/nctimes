Nikhil Chandra closed this year's Summit with a talk on what he calls "boring growth" — the unglamorous kind that compounds quietly instead of arriving as a headline.

The talk leaned on three anonymised case studies, deliberately stripped of the dramatic turnaround narrative most stage talks reach for. The point was to describe what growth actually looks like in practice, rather than what it looks like in a presentation about growth.

The core argument was simple: most companies that describe themselves as trying to grow are actually trying to avoid the conversation about what is preventing growth. The constraint conversation is uncomfortable. Growth feels more optimistic. This preference for the comfortable narrative over the useful diagnosis is, in his account, the main reason most growth initiatives fail.

The recording was not made public at the request of one of the anonymised clients whose case appeared in the talk. A written version is available on request.
