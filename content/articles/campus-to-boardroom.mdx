Long before TheNCTimes existed, Nikhil Chandra spent his first five working years moving between operations floors and client war-rooms, learning — slowly, then all at once — what actually moves a business forward.

The frameworks came later. What came first was a habit of asking the question nobody on the call wanted to answer, usually about ten minutes before the meeting was supposed to end.

By year three, he was running his own mandates. By year five, he had stopped calling himself junior anything.

The first project that failed was also the most useful. Not because failure is instructive in the abstract, but because that particular failure had a specific, fixable cause that took him eighteen months to understand — and has not repeated itself since.

<InfoBox title="Career Timeline">
- **2019–2020** — Operations analyst, first exposure to client-facing work
- **2020–2021** — Junior strategy associate, first independent client contact
- **2021–2022** — Led first solo mandate end-to-end
- **2022 onward** — Independent consulting practice, growth strategy
</InfoBox>

Most of what he knows about consulting he learned in those five years, in the gaps between what he was taught and what the room actually needed. The rest he has been learning since, one engagement at a time, in the same way.
