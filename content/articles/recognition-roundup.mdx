From a podcast feature to a quiet citation in an industry report — five mentions Nikhil Chandra didn't pitch for, and only found out about after the fact.

He keeps a log of these not from vanity but from genuine interest: which work gets cited, and by whom, is a more honest signal about what is actually useful than any self-reported metric. The pattern over the past year has been consistent — the diagnostic methodology gets cited far more than the client outcomes, which suggests it has become a reference point in a way the individual engagements have not.

The five mentions, in no particular order: a podcast feature on mid-cap growth strategy; an unprompted citation in a manufacturing sector resilience report; a quote in a regional business publication on the mentorship cohort; a brief appearance in a market entry guide for Southeast Asian brands entering India; and a mention in a university business school case study that he has not yet been able to read in full because it is behind a paywall.

He was asked whether the mention in the case study was a problem. He said it was not, that he appreciated the citation, and that he hoped the paywall came down eventually so he could find out what they said.
