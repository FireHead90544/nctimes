A short, honest word on why this portfolio exists at all — and what it owes to every actual newspaper sitting, unread, on a desk somewhere this morning.

Most portfolios are built to be skimmed in eleven seconds. This one is built on the bet that a reader who is asked, politely, to turn a page will sometimes turn it — and that the work documented here deserves at least that much patience.

The newspaper format is not nostalgia. It is a set of constraints that produce better writing than the alternatives: a kicker that has to name the thing before the headline does; a deck that has to tell you what the article argues before you read it; a byline that makes someone responsible for what is claimed; a dateline that places the story in time. These constraints exist because newspapers, at their best, are designed to be trustworthy. The format imports that design intent.

<PullQuote cite="Nikhil Chandra, Editor">
A portfolio that has to be written as a dispatch a stranger would want to finish reading will contain different things than one that only has to look impressive on first glance.
</PullQuote>

The editorial test for any new entry is simple: could a stranger who has never heard of Nikhil Chandra pick this up and want to read to the end? If no, it doesn't go in. This test has killed several entries that would have been fine by the standards of most portfolios. It has also produced better entries than would otherwise exist.

Feedback on the format is genuinely welcome. Feedback on the content is also welcome, especially corrections. TheNCTimes has no corrections column yet, which means it has no corrections to print. This seems unlikely to remain true forever.

*— N.C., Editor, TheNCTimes*
