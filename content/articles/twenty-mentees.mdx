Every January, Nikhil Chandra opens twenty seats in a year-long mentorship cohort for early-career consultants — unpaid, oversubscribed, and by his own account, the part of the job he protects hardest against everything else on his calendar.

The curriculum is the same growth diagnostic he uses with paying clients, taught in reverse: mentees practice on each other's hypothetical businesses before ever sitting across from a real one.

The application process is simple: a short note on what constraint the applicant is currently working on, professionally or personally, and what they have already tried. Applicants who describe the constraint clearly and honestly tend to get in. Applicants who describe what they hope to learn from Nikhil Chandra tend not to.

<InfoBox title="Cohort Details">
- **Opens** — Each January
- **Seats** — 20 (oversubscribed)
- **Duration** — Year-long commitment
- **Cost** — No charge
- **Curriculum** — The five-step growth diagnostic
- **Application** — Describe your current constraint
</InfoBox>

Several former mentees now work with him directly — not as employees, but as a loose network of practitioners who share a common diagnostic language and a shared habit of asking the question nobody wants to answer. This was not planned. It happened because the framework travels.

The cohort does not guarantee employment, introductions, or referrals. It guarantees a year of working through real constraints with a small group of people who are serious about learning to do so properly. For the people who want that, the application, when it opens, fills in days.
