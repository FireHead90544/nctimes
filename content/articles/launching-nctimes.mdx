Nikhil Chandra, the New Delhi-based growth consultant, today unveiled TheNCTimes — a personal portfolio that abandons the conventional scrolling website in favour of something closer to a printing press than a product page. Visitors do not scroll. They turn pages.

Every entry, from early career milestones to flagship client engagements, has been typeset as a dated, bylined dispatch, complete with kickers, decks and cross-references to other pages — the full apparatus of a real newsroom, repurposed for a portfolio of one.

<PullQuote cite="Nikhil Chandra, Founding Note">
I wanted a portfolio that felt like it had been put to bed by an editor at midnight, not generated by a template.
</PullQuote>

<Photo variant="ph-b" label="Photo: First Proof, June 2026" caption="The first typeset proof of TheNCTimes front page, reviewed the night before launch." alt="First proof of TheNCTimes" />

The format is deliberately unhurried. A reader who only wants the headline gets a headline; a reader who wants the whole story can open it, read the summary, and decide whether to go further — exactly as a newspaper has always worked, and exactly as most portfolios, with their wall of identical cards, do not.

The choice of form is also a statement of practice. Nikhil Chandra advises businesses on growth. A portfolio that grows by adding dispatches — each one dated, filed under a section, cross-referenced to related work — is itself a demonstration of the discipline he asks of clients.

There are no testimonials. There are no ratings out of five. There is, instead, a record: forty-odd engagements documented as the things they were, in the tense they happened, with the details that actually mattered left in and the ones that didn't left out.

TheNCTimes publishes irregularly, as real things happen, not on a content calendar. New editions will be added as new work is completed and worth writing about. Back copies are available from the first page.
